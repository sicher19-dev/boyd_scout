The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the bus ride, these do.

1

The changeup is the engine tomorrow, not the fastball. Against righties this year the change gets a whiff on 28% of swings; the four-seam gets 17% and comes back off the barrel at 95 mph when they touch it. The fastball's job is to be seen up — the changeup's job is to finish down. Same tunnel, 14 mph apart.

2

The curveball is free strikes. 40.5% called-strike-plus-whiff against righties and almost nothing on contact (.138 xwOBA). This lineup takes early — drop it in first pitch and get to 0-1 without spending the fastball.

3

Lefties are already beaten — stay the course. .200 average, .547 OPS against this season. The slider does it: 49% whiff against left-handed bats. Lowe and O'Hearn get a steady diet of it down and in off the plate.

4

Esmerlyn Valdez is the game. 1.508 OPS and 7 homers in 46 plate appearances against lefties. He hits lefty fastballs at 101 mph and slugs over 1.100 on lefty sliders. But he whiffs on 69% of his swings at changeups from southpaws. One rule, three times: changeup, changeup, changeup — and never a slider. The swing data says why it works: the fastest bat on the team (75 mph) steepens to 23° uphill against offspeed — the changeup falls out of a tunnel his path can't bend down to reach.

5

Second time through, hide the heater. First-look fastball contact comes back at 92.6 mph. Second look: 100.2 mph. Flip the script in innings 3–5 — breaking ball first, fastball as the surprise, not the appetizer.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

Tunnel

Fastball up establishes the line, changeup finishes below it. Against a seven-righty lineup that includes three genuine changeup victims (Valdez, Ozuna, N. Gonzales), the FF/CH pairing is the identity of the night. Target 30–35% changeup usage to righties. The bat paths make it structural: Valdez (23°), Ozuna (24°), Lowe (25°) and Gonzales (19°) all swing steeply uphill at offspeed — the 3.6" ride changeup lives under every one of those planes.

Steal

First-pitch curveball early and often. It's already 25% of first pitches to righties and this lineup's bottom third can't touch it. Bank 0-1, save the fastball's bullets for when they matter.

Red Box

The Valdez rule overrides everything. Changeups down-away and four-seams above the eyes. No sliders — not to steal a strike, not 0-2, not ever. If first base is open in a leverage spot, four wide ones is the right pitch.

Flip

Second time through, breaking-first sequencing. Fastball contact quality jumps 8 mph on the second look. Innings 3–5: open at-bats with curve/change, use the four-seam as the surprise up. The velocity is back (93.4+ in recent starts) — it plays best when it's least expected.

Expand

The bottom third and bench get nothing to hit. Cook, Davis, Mangum and Flores combine for extreme chase and near-zero walks. Every 0-0 to slots 8–9 can start off the plate. Free outs live there — take all of them.

Elevate

The high four-seam is a targeted weapon, not a blanket one. Ladder it to the steep swings — Valdez (11° vs FB), Lowe (10°), Cook (9°), Davis (10°) — where 12.4" of carry beats the uphill path. Skip it against the flat swings: Triolo (2°), Mangum (-4°), N. Gonzales (4°) and Reynolds (1°) cover the top of the zone with the barrel, and elevating to them just spends pitches.

Wall

Respect the Clemente Wall with the lefty bats. Lowe and O'Hearn's pull-side power plays to PNC's short right field. Sliders down-in and ladders up beat them; anything middle-in on a line is a souvenir. The left-center notch is the friend — let righties hit the big part of the park.
